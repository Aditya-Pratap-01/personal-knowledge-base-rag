Book Notes: Atomic Habits by James Clear

Core idea: Small 1% improvements compound over time into remarkable results.
Habits are the compound interest of self-improvement.

The Four Laws of Behavior Change:
1. Make it obvious (cue) - use habit stacking, design your environment.
2. Make it attractive (craving) - pair habits with something you enjoy (temptation bundling).
3. Make it easy (response) - reduce friction, use the two-minute rule to start small.
4. Make it satisfying (reward) - use habit trackers, immediate rewards reinforce behavior.

Identity-based habits: focus on who you wish to become rather than the outcome.
"Every action is a vote for the type of person you wish to become."

Habit stacking formula: "After [current habit], I will [new habit]."

Notes to self: try applying the two-minute rule to gym habit - just put on gym clothes
and drive there, don't worry about the full workout initially. Also try environment
design - keep phone in another room while working.
