Notes on Garhwal Region History

Garhwal region in present-day Uttarakhand was historically ruled by the Garhwal
Kingdom, founded around the 8th-9th century and later unified by King Ajay Pal
in the 15th century who brought together many small forts (the word "Garhwal"
comes from "garh" meaning fort).

The region is known for its Himalayan geography, with important pilgrimage
sites like Badrinath and Kedarnath located within it, forming part of the
Char Dham circuit.

Traditional Garhwali culture has its own dialect (Garhwali), distinct folk
music and dance forms like the Choliya and Langvir Nritya, and terraced
agriculture adapted to the hilly terrain.

The region became part of British India after the Anglo-Gurkha War (early
19th century), and later merged into the state of Uttar Pradesh post-independence
before Uttarakhand was carved out as a separate state in the year 2000.
